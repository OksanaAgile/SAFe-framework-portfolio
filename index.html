1. Achieving Business Agility

Responding rapidly to market changes and delivering innovative digital solutions

All organizations need to move quickly and innovate to win. However, many organizations struggle with Agile labels while acting in traditional ways. Achieving true Lean-Agile benefits requires changing how organizations and teams operate.

Leadership must set strategic objectives while avoiding micromanagement. SAFe provides a structured mechanism to align multiple Scrum teams, manage dependencies, and coordinate across all layers of the enterprise.

Key Framework Objectives

  • Regular rhythm for planning and execution across ARTs
  • Structured alignment between business strategy and data execution
  • Accelerated delivery with built-in quality and continuous feedback

2. The Foundation Layer

The cultural and philosophical bedrock of SAFe 6.0

1. Alignment

Everyone works toward identical strategic outcomes, connecting high-level strategy directly to execution.

2. Transparency

Making all work, risks, and progress visible across squads, eliminating silos, and building trust.

3. Respect for People

Valuing individuals, autonomy, and cultural diversity with relationships built on mutual trust.

4. Relentless Improvement

Constant reflection, root-cause analysis, and optimizing processes and technical capabilities every sprint.

The Lean-Agile Mindset

Combines beliefs, assumptions, and actions of leaders embracing the Agile Manifesto and Lean thinking.

  • House of Lean: Focuses on value, respect for people and culture, flow, innovation, and relentless improvement.
  • Agile Manifesto: Emphasizes working software/data increments, customer collaboration, and adaptive planning over rigid processes.

The 10 SAFe Principles

1. Take an economic view
2. Apply systems thinking
3. Assume variability; preserve options
4. Build incrementally with fast learning cycles
5. Base milestones on working systems
6. Make value flow without interruptions
7. Apply cadence & cross-domain planning
8. Unlock knowledge worker motivation
9. Decentralize decision-making
10. Organize around value

3. The Seven Core Competencies of SAFe 6.0

Essential enterprise capabilities required to achieve Business Agility

  • 1. Team & Technical Agility: High-performing cross-functional squads and built-in quality.
  • 2. Agile Product Delivery (APD): Customer-centric value delivery through continuous delivery pipelines.
  • 3. Enterprise Solution Delivery: Managing complex multi-train systems and cloud/data infrastructure.
Strategy & Alignment
  • 4. Lean Portfolio Management (LPM): Strategy, funding, governance, and agile portfolio operations.
  • 5. Organizational Agility: Strategy orientation and quick adaptation to market shifts.
  • 6. Continuous Learning Culture: Learning organization, relentless improvement, and innovation culture.
Foundation
  • 7. Lean-Agile Leadership: Leaders as lean-thinking managers and teachers who model core behaviors and lead change.

Guiding artifacts and roles applied across team, program, and portfolio contexts

The Solution Vision defines the future state answering "Where are we going?" and "Why?". OKRs connect portfolio strategy to team PI objectives with quantifiable Key Results.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Highlighted in SAFe 6 as a transformative tool applied to Intelligent Solutions (LLMs/ML), Value Stream Automation (CI/CD and code gen), and Operations & Insights.

The System Team

Specialized Agile Team supporting the ART by building pipeline automation, cloud infrastructure, end-to-end integration testing, and staging environment maintenance.

5. Cadence, Roles & Data Team Artifacts

Structuring execution from 2-week iterations to 8-12 week Planning Intervals (PIs)

Level Key Roles Core Responsibilities
Portfolio Level Epic Owners, Enterprise Architect Portfolio strategy, strategic themes, Lean budgets, and epic governance.
ART / Train Level Business Owners, Product Management, System Architect, RTE Multi-team synchronization, PI Planning execution, and continuous delivery pipelines.
Agile Team Level Product Owner (PO), Scrum Master / Team Coach, Agile Team (Engineers/QA) 2-week iteration execution, backlog refinement with BDD, and building data increments.

Team-Level Data Artifacts

  • Team Backlog: Prioritized work items managed via WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First).
  • User Stories & Enablers: Functional (PO Happy Path) and technical enablers with `Given-When-Then` BDD.
  • Data Contracts: Explicit schema specifications, target types (e.g., VARCHAR, NUMBER), and nullability rules attached to DoR.
  • Iteration Goals: 1-sentence commitment for the 2-week sprint.

The 2-Week Sprint Cadence

  • Day 1: Iteration Planning & Capacity Check against Definition of Ready (DoR).
  • Days 2–9: Pipeline development, dbt modeling, Daily Standup (walking board right-to-left), and mid-sprint refinement.
  • Day 10: Iteration Review (Demo vs. BDD) & Retrospective for continuous improvement.
